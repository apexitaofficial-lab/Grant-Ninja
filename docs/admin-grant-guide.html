Adding and managing grants by hand

Most grants arrive automatically, collected from government websites by the crawler. This guide covers the other kind — the ones you enter yourself, for countries and programmes the crawler cannot reach.

Finding the right page

Everything in this guide happens in the admin panel, under Grants in the left-hand menu. You need to be signed in as an editor or an administrator.

To do thisGo here
Add a new grantGrants → Add a grant (top right)
Change an existing grantGrants → click the grant's title
Remove a grantGrants → click the title → Delete (top right)
See what is waiting for a decisionGrants → Needs review tab

The steps are the same for every country. What changes between the USA, the UK and Italy is covered in one section further down rather than repeated three times — the form does not behave differently, it just offers different choices.

Adding a grant

You will need the grant's official page open in another tab. Everything you enter should come from that page.

  1. Go to Grants and press "Add a grant".

  2. Choose the country first.

    This has to come first because it decides everything below it: which agencies you can pick, which regions exist, and which currency the awards are recorded in. The currency appears under the field so you can check it — USD for the United States, GBP for the United Kingdom, EUR for Italy.

  3. Choose the funding agency.

    The list only shows agencies for the country you picked. If the agency is not there — which will be the case for most UK and Italian grants at first — choose "+ Add a new agency…" and type its full official name. You can add its website too, though that is optional.

    Type the name carefully. It becomes a page on the public site, and every grant you file under it afterwards will use the same entry.

  4. Set the funding level.

    Three choices: national government, regional or local government, or a foundation or private funder. This is what drives the federal / state / private filter on the public site, so getting it wrong makes the grant hard to find rather than invisible.

  5. Tick at least one category.

    The first one you tick becomes the primary category — the one shown in the breadcrumb trail on the grant's page. You can change which is primary by pressing "make primary" next to another ticked category.

  6. Write the title and descriptions.

    Use the official title. Write the descriptions in your own words rather than pasting the whole notice — a summary you wrote is more useful to a reader and avoids duplicating the agency's page.

  7. Enter the award amounts and dates.

    Leave anything the notice does not state empty. An empty field reads as "not published"; a zero reads as "this grant awards nothing".

  8. Paste the official URL.

    The agency's own page for this grant. This is required before the grant can go live — it is how a reader checks us, and it is the link the page points them to.

  9. Choose a status and press "Create grant".

    Draft saves it without publishing — use this if you are part-way through. Pending review queues it for someone to check. Published puts it on the public site straight away.

Good to know

You do not choose the web address. It is generated from the title, and made unique automatically if another grant already has that name.

If the form will not save

Two rules are enforced when you choose Published: there must be an official URL, and at least one category. If either is missing you will see a message next to the field. Save it as a draft instead, fill in what is missing, then publish.

Editing a grant

Open Grants, find the grant and click its title. The page is split into parts, and each saves on its own.

Part of the pageWhat it changes
Decision Publish, unpublish or archive the grant
Filing Country, agency, region, funding level and categories
Details Title, descriptions, eligibility, amounts, dates and links

  1. Change what you need to, in the relevant part.

    Correcting a deadline is a "Details" change. Moving a grant to the right ministry is a "Filing" change. They are separate because they are different kinds of decision, and each is recorded separately in the history.

  2. Write a short reason.

    Optional, but it is what makes the history readable in six months. "Corrected the closing date from the notice" tells the next person what happened; a blank does not.

  3. Press Save.

    Each part has its own save button and only saves its own fields. If you changed both the filing and the details, press both.

Nothing is lost

Every save keeps a full copy of what the grant looked like beforehand, along with who changed it and when. You can see the trail under History at the bottom of the page.

Changing the country

Changing a grant's country clears the agency and region, because neither exists in the new country. You will need to pick them again before you can save.

Deleting a grant

Open the grant and press Delete at the top right. You will be asked to confirm, and to give a reason.

What happens: the grant disappears from the public site, from search results on the site, and from the sitemap that search engines read. The record itself is kept, along with its full history.

This can be undone

Because the record is kept rather than destroyed, an administrator can restore a grant deleted by mistake. Ask your developer — it is not something the panel does on its own.

Consider archiving instead

If a grant has simply closed, Archive is the better choice. It also removes the grant from public view, but it says "this programme ended" rather than "this should not have been here". Delete is for genuine mistakes and duplicates.

Deleting requires the administrator role. Editors can archive but not delete.

USA, UK and Italy

Adding, editing and deleting work identically in all three. The differences are only in what you will find waiting for you in the dropdowns.

United States

Currency
USD, set automatically
Agencies
Around 150 already listed, collected from Grants.gov. Yours is probably there.
Region field
Shown. Choose a state, or leave it on "Whole country" for a federal programme.
Funding level
"National government" for a federal grant; "State, regional or local" for a state one.

United Kingdom

Currency
GBP, set automatically
Agencies
None yet. Use "+ Add a new agency" — e.g. Innovate UK, UKRI, or the relevant department.
Region field
Hidden, because no UK regions are recorded. Grants apply UK-wide.
Funding level
"National government" for anything from a UK department or research council.

Italy

Currency
EUR, set automatically
Agencies
None yet. Use "+ Add a new agency" — e.g. Ministero dell'Università e della Ricerca.
Region field
Hidden, because no Italian regions are recorded. Grants apply nationally.
Funding level
"National government" for a ministry; "Foundation or private funder" for a fondazione.
The first grant is the slow one

For the UK and Italy you will create the agency the first time you add a grant. Every grant after that can simply pick it from the list.

Nothing here is limited to these three countries. Any country in the list works the same way, and adding a new one is a small job for your developer rather than a rebuild.

Field reference

FieldHow to fill it in
Country required Pick first. Sets the currency and filters the agency and region lists.
Funding agency required Choose an existing one, or "+ Add a new agency" and type the full official name.
Title required The official name of the grant. Three characters minimum.
Categories to publish At least one. The first ticked becomes the primary.

State or region optional Only appears where the country has regions recorded. Leave on "Whole country" otherwise.
Short description optional One or two sentences. This is what appears on cards and in search results.
Full description optional The detail. Write it rather than pasting the notice wholesale.
Eligibility optional Who can apply. Readers look for this before anything else.
Minimum / maximum award optional Numbers only, no currency symbol or commas. Leave empty if unstated — do not enter 0. The minimum cannot exceed the maximum.

Dates matter more than anything else here

A wrong closing date is the one error that actively harms someone — they plan around it and miss the real deadline. Check it against the official notice before you publish, not after.

After you save

Saving a grant as Published sets off several things at once:

  • The grant appears on the public site at its own address.
  • It joins the listings for its country, category and agency, and their counts go up.
  • It becomes findable through search and the filters.
  • It is added to the sitemap that search engines read.
  • Its structured data is generated automatically — you do not enter any of it.

Checking it worked

  1. From the grant's page in the admin panel, note the title.

  2. Open the public site and search for it, or go to Browse grants and filter by its country.

  3. Open the grant and check the four things a reader acts on: the award, the closing date, who can apply, and that the "Read the official notice" link goes where it should.

If it is not there yet

Pages are cached for a short period. If a change does not show immediately, refresh once after a minute. If it still is not there, check the status is Published rather than Draft — that is the usual explanation.

Checking the SEO data

Every published grant automatically carries structured data — the machine-readable summary search engines use to understand the page. It is generated from what you typed, so there is nothing extra to fill in. Checking it is worth doing once for a new grant.

  1. Open Google's Rich Results Test.

  2. Paste the grant's public web address and press Test URL.

  3. Wait for it to finish, then look at what it detected. You should see several items listed, including a breadcrumb trail and the grant itself.

What you might seeWhat it means
Items detected, no errors Working as intended.
"Not eligible for rich results" Normal, and not a fault. It means Google will not draw a special box in search results for that type. The data is still valid and still read.
A warning about a missing field Usually a field left empty on the grant — an image, or a date. Fill it in if the official notice gives you one; leave it if it does not.
An error, or nothing detected Worth reporting. Send the grant's address to your developer.
Draft grants cannot be tested

The test fetches the live page, so a grant has to be published before it will find anything. A draft returns "page not found", which is correct rather than broken.

If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat to do
"A published grant needs an official URL" Paste the agency's page for the grant into Official URL, or save it as a draft instead.
"A published grant needs at least one category" Tick a category. If none fit, use "Others".
"The closing date cannot come before the opening date" One of the two dates is wrong. Check both against the notice.
"Choose an existing agency or enter a new one" You picked "+ Add a new agency" but left the name blank.
The agency dropdown is empty Expected for a country with no agencies yet. Use "+ Add a new agency".
"Not authorised" Deleting needs the administrator role. Ask an administrator, or archive instead.
One thing not to do

Do not re-enter a grant that already exists to fix a mistake in it. Edit the existing one. Creating a second copy leaves two pages competing for the same search results, and the older one keeps its links.
